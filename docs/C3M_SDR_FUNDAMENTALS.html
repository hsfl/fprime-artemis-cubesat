How does our software-defined radio actually work?

A radio wave nudges a point around a circle. HackRF records the point’s x and y coordinates millions of times per second. Our software turns those coordinates into bits, frames, messages, and finally the same byte streams F´ GDS already understands.

Level 0 · Feynman version

The one-minute explanation

A normal radio has behavior baked into chips. An SDR moves much of that behavior into software. HackRF owns the fast analog↔digital boundary; Python decides what the numbers mean.

1. Air carries a wiggle

The satellite shifts a 433 MHz carrier slightly up or down. Those shifts encode 1s and 0s: GFSK.

2. HackRF measures it

HackRF converts a slice of spectrum into signed I/Q number pairs and streams them over USB.

3. Software recognizes it

We measure phase change, decide bits, find sync, check CRC, join segments, and output the original bytes.

If you can teach those three cards without saying “magic,” you understand the core of SDR.

Level 1 · What HackRF gives us

I/Q is a clock hand’s coordinates

Imagine a rotating clock hand. At each instant record horizontal position I and vertical position Q. Together, I + jQ preserve amplitude and phase.

complex sample = I + jQ   ·   phase = atan2(Q, I)

Two coordinates preserve rotation direction, allowing software to distinguish positive and negative frequency offsets.

Our numbers

HackRF samples 8,000,000 complex samples/second. I and Q are signed 8-bit values (cs8): about 16 MB/s raw. We decimate by 16 to 500,000 samples/s—four samples per 125,000-symbol/s bit.

Tune and shrink

The receiver digitally mixes the desired carrier toward zero frequency, groups 16 samples, and averages. This isolates the known signal and cuts work 16×.

Motion becomes signs

For neighbors x[n] and x[n−1], calculate angle(x[n]·conj(x[n−1])). That phase change is proportional to instantaneous frequency. Remove median bias; positive/negative becomes a candidate bit.

Recognize a real frame

Try four symbol phases, correlate against sync 2D D4, then require downlink identity A1 A2 C3 01, legal length, and correct RF22 CRC-16. Noise may resemble bits; passing every gate is much harder.

Undo segmentation

RF22 payload is at most 49 bytes here. Five segment-header bytes leave 44 data bytes. Check channel magic, message ID, index/count, and length; reject stale, malformed, duplicate, or out-of-order pieces; emit only complete messages.

Pretend to be the ground Teensy

Write complete messages to software serial devices: /tmp/c3m-sdr/gds-port (channel 0) and /tmp/c3m-sdr/payload-port (channel 1). Consumers need not know whether a Teensy or SDR supplied the bytes.

How a bit becomes frequency

Map bits to −1/+1. A Gaussian filter (BT=0.5) smooths transitions. Shaped symbols select about −125/+125 kHz deviation. Integrate frequency into phase; evaluate 90·ejφ; quantize to signed I/Q.

Why the radio takes turns

HackRF One is half-duplex. The bridge stops RX, transmits, then restores RX. It may miss a fast RF ACK during USB turnaround, so TX calibration waits for a delayed exact F´ PING/Pong token—proof of uplink, satellite execution, and downlink.

Channel 0 · A5

Opaque F´/CCSDS stream to gds-port. GDS uses space-packet-space-data-link. Ordinary uplink gets RF segment ACK/retry.

Channel 1 · A6

Payload/preview/science bytes to payload-port. RF segments are ACK-free; application CRC and selective repair own completeness.

Channel 2 is satellite-local Teensy RPC and never crosses RF.

Level 4 · Reliability and proof

Every gate removes a different wrong

Gain

Too little loses bits in noise; too much clips 8-bit I/Q. RX and TX each search normal gain first. Only a complete failure enables that direction's RF amplifier and restarts from minimum gain; the proven settings then freeze for the session. Passive-antenna bias stays off.

Sync + identity

Sync finds boundaries. TO/FROM/network/version reject other traffic. They do not prove who sent it.

CRC

RF CRC rejects bad frames. Application CRC checks reconstructed science/preview. Neither is cryptographic authentication.

Ordering

IDs and indexes expose missing, duplicate, reordered, and stale pieces. Reassembly timeout is 500 ms.

Backpressure

PTY queues are bounded. A stopped consumer becomes a visible failure, not unbounded memory growth.

Real proof

Green UI is insufficient. Require RF frame counters, exact Pong, complete packet count, final CRC, decode, and saved artifacts.

Level 5 · Full detail

The math without hand-waving

1. Baseband sampling

We do not sample a 433 MHz sinusoid at 8 MHz directly. HackRF’s RF chain and oscillator translate a selected band to complex baseband. Complex sampling represents offsets around the tuned center.

2. Digital mixing

Multiply by e−j2πf_offset n/Fs to rotate a desired offset toward zero Hz. Live mode tunes directly; offline decoding also supports 432.5 MHz center with 433 MHz at +500 kHz.

3. Decimation

Mix and average groups of 16 samples: 8 MHz becomes 500 kHz, or four samples/symbol. This is a deliberately simple receiver matched to the known waveform, not a universal modem.

4. FM discriminator

If x[n]=A[n]ejφ[n], then x[n]·conj(x[n−1]) has phase φ[n]−φ[n−1]. Its angle estimates instantaneous frequency. Median removal handles steady offset; sign proposes bits.

5. Timing and validation

Try four sample phases, correlate with 16 sync bits, parse the 4-byte identity and length, then compute CRC-16/IBM (poly 0x8005, MSB-first, init/xorout 0).

6. Inverse transmit path

Serialize MSB-first; prepend 32 alternating preamble bits and sync; map bits to ±1; Gaussian-shape across four symbols; multiply by 125 kHz deviation; integrate into phase; make complex samples; quantize cs8; stream to HackRF.

“Software-defined” does not mean “all software”

Antenna, filters, amplifiers, mixers, oscillator, ADC/DAC, USB timing, and RF limits remain physical. Software defines much of the modem and protocol between digitization and conversion back to analog.
